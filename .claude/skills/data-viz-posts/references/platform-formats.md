# Platform formats and review

## X/Twitter

Write one post under 280 characters when it can carry the takeaway. Use
a thread only when each post has one indispensable idea and post one can
stand alone. State which visual goes with the post. Provide concise alt
text that conveys the result, not just colours or layout.

## Substack

Provide a title, subtitle, then 300 to 700 words of flowing prose. Use
few headers and bullets. Put each figure beside a one-line caption that
adds information not already in the paragraph. End with a short source,
method, or limitations note.

## Quality score, 10 points

- 2: Main takeaway is specific and supported.
- 2: Data source, scope, and time frame are accurate.
- 2: Reader can interpret the visual and its units.
- 1: Caveat is proportionate and clear.
- 1: Tone is direct, plain, and has no em dash.
- 1: No redundancy after sentence-job check.
- 1: Tags and hashtags are relevant or intentionally omitted.

Score below 8: revise before publishing. Explain deductions in the
editor note and offer one concrete improvement.

## Examples

### X input

Chart: a line chart of 231 recorded Modi approval-related observations,
May 2019 to June 2024. Sources are mixed and not harmonized. Audience:
general readers.

### X output

How did measures of approval for Narendra Modi change across his second
term? This chart connects 231 recorded observations from May 2019 to
June 2024. Because it combines sources, it shows broad movement rather
than one pollster’s trend.

Visual: the approval-measures line chart.

Alt text: Line chart of 231 mixed-source approval-related observations
for Narendra Modi between May 2019 and June 2024. The series varies over
time; it is not a single harmonized polling series.

Score: 10/10. No tags or hashtags suggested without a specific relevant
partner account.

### Substack input

Dataset: 10 India Today–Karvy government-performance observations from
July 2016 to August 2025. Audience: students.

### Substack output

Title: Measuring views of government performance in India

Subtitle: Ten Mood of the Nation observations show a changing but
infrequent series from 2016 to 2025.

The chart tracks positive ratings of government performance in the
India Today–Karvy Mood of the Nation series. It is useful because it
separates views of the government from approval of an individual prime
minister, two measures that can move differently.

Read the line from left to right. The horizontal axis shows the survey
date, while the vertical axis shows the share giving a positive rating.
The series rises to 72% in July 2020, then records 52.4% in August
2025. With only ten observations, the chart cannot show every short-run
change between surveys.

The main lesson is about measurement: government performance should not
be substituted for personal approval. Keeping the two series separate
makes comparisons more honest and easier to interpret.

Figure caption: Positive government-performance ratings in ten
India Today–Karvy survey observations, not personal approval ratings.

Source and limitation: Pollitik reference data, India Today–Karvy
Insights Mood of the Nation. The series is intermittent, so it is not a
continuous tracker.

Score: 10/10. The earlier draft’s repeated distinction between
government and personal approval was reduced to one explanatory sentence
and one caption.
